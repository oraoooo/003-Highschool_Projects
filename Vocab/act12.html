Making Every Drop Count - Vocabulary

1. entwine /ɪnˈtwaɪn/

Definition: To be very closely involved or connected with something.

Sentence: The history of human civilization is entwined with the history of the ways we have learned to manipulate water resources.

2. aqueduct /ˈæk.wə.dʌkt/

Definition: A structure for carrying water across land, especially a bridge-like structure with arches.

Sentence: As towns gradually expanded, water was brought from increasingly remote sources, leading to sophisticated engineering efforts such as dams and aqueducts.

3. unprecedented /ʌnˈprɛsɪˌdɛntɪd/

Definition: Never having happened or existed in the past.

Sentence: The 19th and 20th centuries saw unprecedented construction of monumental engineering projects designed to control floods and provide water for irrigation.

4. irrigation /ˌɪrɪˈɡeɪʃən/

Definition: The artificial application of water to land to aid in growing crops.

Sentence: Food production has kept pace with soaring populations mainly because of the expansion of artificial irrigation systems.

5. sanitation /ˌsænɪˈteɪʃən/

Definition: The process of keeping places clean and hygienic.

Sentence: More than two and a half billion people do not have adequate sanitation services.

6. reservoir /ˈrezəvwɑːr/

Definition: A large natural or artificial lake used as a source of water supply.

Sentence: Tens of millions of people have been forced to move from their homes to make way for the reservoirs behind dams.

7. aquifer /ˈækwɪfə/

Definition: A body of permeable rock that can contain or transmit groundwater.

Sentence: Groundwater aquifers are being pumped down faster than they are naturally replenished.

8. degradation /ˌdɛɡrəˈdeɪʃən/

Definition: The process by which something becomes worse or is damaged.

Sentence: Certain irrigation practices degrade soil quality and cause degradation of agricultural productivity.

9. replenished /rɪˈplɛnɪʃt/

Definition: To fill something up again.

Sentence: Aquifers are being pumped down faster than they are naturally replenished.

10. hydropower /ˈhaɪdroʊˌpaʊər/

Definition: Electricity produced using the energy of moving water.

Sentence: Nearly one-fifth of all the electricity worldwide is generated by hydropower.

11. ecological /ˌiːkəˈlɒdʒɪkəl/

Definition: Relating to the relationships between living organisms and their environment.

Sentence: New water projects must respect ecological criteria and smaller budgets.

12. contamination /kənˌtæmɪˈneɪʃən/

Definition: The process of making something dirty, polluted, or harmful.

Sentence: Clean drinking water is crucial for reducing the risk of contamination.

13. dispute /dɪˈspjuːt/

Definition: A disagreement, argument, or debate.

Sentence: Disputes over shared water resources have led to violence in many areas.
